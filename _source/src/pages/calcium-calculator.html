      Why calcium and vitamin D matter

      About 99 percent of the body's calcium is stored in bones and teeth. When you don't get enough from food, your body draws on that store to keep blood levels steady, and over years that can thin your bones. Vitamin D helps your gut absorb calcium, so the two work as a team.

      Easy ways to hit your target

      • Spread it out. A calcium-rich food at each meal is easier than one big serving, and your body absorbs smaller amounts better.
      • Look beyond dairy. Calcium-set tofu, canned sardines or salmon with bones, fortified plant milks and juices, and greens such as kale and bok choy all count.
      • Read fortified labels. Calcium content varies widely between brands of plant milk and juice. Shake cartons, because added calcium can settle.
      • Don't count on spinach. It contains calcium, but very little of it is absorbed.
