How the quiz works

Each round asks ten questions drawn at random from five types: where a bone sits, how many of it you have, which bone goes by a common name such as the kneecap or collarbone, which bone does a particular job, and how many bones a whole region holds. After every answer you'll see a short explanation, so even a wrong answer teaches you something.

Flashcards cover all 62 bone groups in the 206 Bone Explorer, from the frontal bone to the distal phalanges of the toes. Tap a card to flip it and see its count, region, job and a fact.

Tips for learning the skeleton

  • Learn by region. Master the 22 skull bones, then the spine, then the limbs. The explorer's region chips make this easy.
  • Use the patterns. Hands and feet mirror each other: 14 phalanges in each hand and foot, five metacarpals and five metatarsals.
  • Use mnemonics. Many students remember the eight carpal bones (scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform, trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate) with a short memory sentence that uses the same first letters in the same order.
  • Space your practice. Short daily sessions beat one long cram. The bone of the day is a painless way to keep going.
