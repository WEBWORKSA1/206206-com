      Why calcium and vitamin D matter

      About 99 percent of the body's calcium is stored in bones and teeth. When you don't get enough from food, your body draws on that store to keep blood levels steady, and over years that can thin your bones. Vitamin D helps your gut absorb calcium, so the two work as a team.

      Easy ways to hit your target

      • Spread it out. A calcium-rich food at each meal is easier than one big serving, and your body absorbs smaller amounts better.
      • Look beyond dairy. Calcium-set tofu, canned sardines or salmon with bones, fortified plant milks and juices, and greens such as kale and bok choy all count.
      • Read fortified labels. Calcium content varies widely between brands of plant milk and juice. Shake cartons, because added calcium can settle.
      • Don't count on spinach. It contains calcium, but very little of it is absorbed.

      Questions people ask

      How much calcium do adults need a day?

      According to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, adults 19 to 50 need 1,000 mg a day. Women 51 and older and everyone 71 and older need 1,200 mg, while men 51 to 70 need 1,000 mg.

      How much vitamin D do I need?

      The recommended amount is 600 IU (15 mcg) a day for people aged 1 to 70, and 800 IU (20 mcg) from age 71. Some people need more, so ask your doctor whether your level should be checked.

      Is it better to get calcium from food or supplements?

      Food first is the usual advice. Supplements can fill a gap, but very high intakes, mostly from supplements, are linked to kidney stones and other problems. Talk to a clinician before taking them.

      Can I take all my calcium supplement at once?

      Your body absorbs calcium best in amounts of 500 mg or less at a time, so larger daily amounts are usually split. Calcium carbonate is best taken with food; calcium citrate can be taken with or without food.
